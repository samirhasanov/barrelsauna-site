A well-built sauna can serve you for many years — on one condition: it gets the right care. The short answer: **air and dry the sauna after every use, wash the benches weekly, check the stones once a year, and with an outdoor sauna, inspect the bands, roof and exterior cladding whenever the season changes.** These simple habits prevent mould, musty smells, darkened wood and premature heater wear.

Below is a sauna maintenance routine organised by frequency: after every use, weekly, seasonal and yearly.

## After every use: the five-minute routine

1. **Open the door and vents.** Moisture in the hot room needs somewhere to go.
2. **Dry the room.** Leaving an electric heater on for a while — say 15–30 minutes — with the door ajar dries the wood quickly. With a wood-burning stove, the residual heat is usually enough.
3. **Empty the bucket** and leave the bucket and ladle to dry.
4. **Take out towels, mats and robes** — don't dry them inside the sauna.
5. **Wipe the benches** with a dry or slightly damp towel, especially the sweatiest spots.

## Cleaning benches and interior wood

Linden (lipa), thermo-linden, abachi and cedar — the woods used for sauna interiors — are natural materials that need to breathe. So keep the care gentle.

### Weekly cleaning

- Wash benches, backrests and the floor grid with **warm water and mild soap** or a sauna-specific cleaner.
- Use a **soft brush or sponge**, not a stiff wire brush.
- Always dry the sauna afterwards.

### What not to do

- **Bleach, chlorine and harsh chemicals** damage the wood and can smell unpleasant when heated.
- **Pressure washers** drive water deep into the timber.
- **Varnish or paint inside the hot room** turns sticky when heated, gives off odours and stops the wood from breathing. If you want to treat benches, use only a special paraffin oil made for saunas.

### Dark stains

Sweat marks show up faster on light-coloured linden. The best prevention is to **always sit on a towel**. If a stain appears, lightly sand the dry wood with fine-grit sandpaper and wipe away the dust.

## Ventilation: mould's worst enemy

The faster a sauna dries, the longer it lasts. Good ventilation:

- prevents mould and fungus;
- removes stale, damp smells;
- reduces the risk of wood warping.

If your sauna has vent dampers, leave them open after use. In our saunas, the multilayer wall — mineral stone wool plus a special membrane — keeps moisture out of the wooden frame, but drying the room itself still comes down to your habits.

## Heater and stone care

- **Check the stones at least once a year.** Take them out, discard any that are crumbled or cracked, rinse the rest and restack them loosely. Do this more often with heavy use. You'll find replacement stones under [accessories](/en/sauna-accessories).
- **On an electric heater**, check the heating elements for damage, make sure the temperature sensor is in place and keep the heater clear.
- **On a wood-burning stove**, remove ash regularly and have the chimney swept at least once a year, before the season starts.
- If anything electrical seems wrong, don't take the heater apart yourself — call a qualified technician.

## Caring for the outside of an outdoor sauna

### Exterior cladding

Our saunas are clad outside in water-resistant, thermo-treated larch ("listvenica", also called ship timber). It resists moisture and rot well, but it still deserves attention:

- Once a season, inspect it for cracks, damage and colour changes.
- **Don't let water pool** under the sauna; make sure the base drains.
- Keep plants, shrubs and leaf piles away from the walls.
- If you'd like to apply a protective finish to the exterior, check with us first.

### Re-tightening barrel sauna bands

Wood is a living material: it swells slightly in humid weather and shrinks in dry, hot seasons. That's exactly why our barrel saunas have **adjustable metal bands** — when the wood shrinks, you can tighten the body again.

- Check the bands **at least in spring and autumn**, and after any long dry spell.
- If gaps appear between the staves, tighten the bands **evenly and gradually**.
- **Don't over-tighten** — the wood needs room to expand again when humidity rises.

More about how the barrel is built on our [barrel sauna](/en/barrel-sauna) page.

### Roof checks

Our saunas use tegola bitumen shingles over a strong roofing-felt layer — a double shield; some models use ondulin or profiled metal sheet instead.

- After strong wind or storms, check that all shingles are in place.
- Clear leaves and branches from the roof so water runs off freely.
- On metal sheet and ondulin, check that the screws are tight.
- With a wood-burning stove, check the seal where the chimney passes through the roof.

## Caring for an indoor sauna: what's different

[Indoor saunas](/en/indoor-saunas) are built inside an existing room, with multilayer insulation — mineral stone wool and a moisture-resistant membrane — between the house walls and the sauna frame. That layer protects your home from steam and humidity, but a few habits still matter:

- Make sure **humid air** from the sauna goes into the home's ventilation system or outside, not just into the next room.
- Air out the room that houses the sauna regularly too, especially if it's next to a bathroom.
- Check the **door seal** and hinges from time to time — a warped door lets heat escape.
- If you notice an unusual smell, a damp patch on a wall or mould, don't wait — contact a specialist.

## Winter sauna care

- Clear **heavy snow from the roof** carefully, without damaging the shingles.
- In freezing weather, **drain water lines, showers and hot/cold tubs**, or protect them from freezing.
- **Drying after use matters even more** in winter, when condensation builds up faster.
- Keep the path and entrance clear of snow and ice.

## Sauna maintenance schedule

| Frequency | What to do |
|---|---|
| After every use | Air out, dry, empty the bucket, wipe the benches |
| Weekly | Wash benches and floor with a mild cleaner |
| Each change of season | Check barrel bands, roof and exterior cladding |
| Yearly | Inspect/replace stones, sweep the chimney, have the heater checked |
| Winter | Clear snow, protect water lines from freezing |

## FAQ

### What should I use to clean sauna benches?

Warm water with mild soap or a dedicated sauna cleaner works best. Avoid bleach and chlorine, and always dry the sauna afterwards.

### Can I varnish the inside of my sauna?

No. Varnish and paint turn sticky when heated, give off odours and stop the wood breathing. For benches, only a special sauna paraffin oil is suitable.

### When should I re-tighten the bands on a barrel sauna?

Check them at least every spring and autumn, and after a long dry, hot spell. If gaps appear between the staves, tighten the bands evenly — but don't over-tighten.

### How often should sauna stones be replaced?

Inspect them at least once a year and replace any crumbled stones; more often if the sauna is used a lot.

### What should I do if mould appears in my sauna?

It's usually caused by poor airing and drying. Clean the spot with a mild cleaner, lightly sand the dry wood and make drying the sauna after each use a habit. If it keeps coming back, consult a specialist.
