In regions like Gabala (Qəbələ), Gakh (Qax), Ismayilli (İsmayıllı), Shamakhi (Şamaxı) and Guba (Quba), guests no longer want just a bed and breakfast — they want an **experience**. The short answer: **a sauna for hotels extends the season, gives guests another reason to book, can earn extra revenue as an add-on service and sets your property apart.** The right model depends on scale: a large resort suits an outdoor spa complex, a guest house a cottage sauna, and a glamping site a compact barrel or cube.

Below we look at how a sauna adds value for guests, which model fits which kind of property, and the real projects we've built in the regions.

## Why saunas are such a draw in Azerbaijan's regions

### A longer season

In the mountain regions summer is short, and autumn and winter evenings are cool. That's exactly when a sauna becomes the most requested amenity: hot steam with a view of a snowy forest, then a cold plunge — guests suddenly have a strong reason to visit in the off-season.

### An extra service and revenue line

A sauna can be included in the room rate, rented by the hour or packaged as a "romantic evening" or "family day" offer. It's an extra line of business that doesn't depend only on room occupancy.

### Visibility on social media

A wooden barrel sauna at the edge of the forest, or a cabin sauna with a panoramic glass front, is one of the most-shared shots guests post. Every post is free advertising for your property.

### Standing out

There are plenty of guest houses in the regions, but not all of them offer a sauna. On booking platforms, the word "sauna" is often the detail that tips a guest's decision.

### Outdoor spa complex

This is, quite literally, a "5-star-hotel-level private spa in your yard". One project can combine a sauna room, steam room, salt room, Turkish hammam, broom (venik) room, massage room, hot/cold tub or pool, bathroom and a relaxation room. Ideal for hotels that want to offer a full spa experience.

### Cottage sauna

The house-shaped cottage sauna offers the same set of rooms in a more flexible format. It works well for guest houses: start with a sauna room and lounge, and plan the rest of the layout around your needs.

### Barrel, cube and cabin saunas

For glamping and standalone cottages, compact models make more sense — a private sauna beside each unit is a premium offer in its own right. A cabin sauna turns the view into the main attraction. See every model on our [outdoor saunas](/en/outdoor-saunas) page.

## What matters in a mountain climate

Humidity, rain, snow and big temperature swings demand more from an outdoor sauna. In ours you'll find:

- **Exterior cladding** — water-resistant, thermo-treated red pine "listvenica" (larch), also known as ship timber.
- **Interior lining** — linden (lipa) by default; cedar, abachi or teak on request.
- **Multilayer walls** — mineral stone wool insulation plus a special membrane that keeps moisture and steam away from the wooden frame.
- **Roofing** — tegola bitumen shingles over a strong roofing-felt layer for a double shield; ondulin or profiled metal sheet on other models.
- **Adjustable metal bands** on barrel saunas, so the body can be re-tightened when the wood naturally shrinks with weather changes.

## Planning a hotel sauna: what to consider

1. **Location.** View, guest privacy, distance from the main building and water drainage.
2. **Heater.** An electric heater with a separate control panel lets staff manage temperature and timers; a wood-burning stove creates a traditional atmosphere. More in our [heaters](/en/sauna-heaters) section.
3. **Electrical capacity.** If you're planning several saunas or a spa complex, calculate the load on your supply in advance.
4. **Capacity and guest flow.** How many people will use it at once? Choose the model accordingly.
5. **Rules and safety.** Post usage rules, a clock, drinking water and clear warnings near the sauna.
6. **Maintenance plan.** Make someone responsible for daily cleaning, airing and seasonal checks.

## FAQ

### Which sauna suits a small guest house?

A 2–6 person barrel or cube sauna is the easiest choice for a small guest house or glamping site. If you host more guests, consider a 4–8 person cabin or semi-circular sauna, or a cottage sauna.

### How much does a sauna for a hotel cost?

Approximate ranges: barrel and cube saunas 6,000–12,000 AZN; cabin, semi-circular and elliptical saunas 8,000–15,000 AZN; cottage saunas 10,000–50,000 AZN; outdoor spa complexes 15,000–45,000 AZN. The exact price depends on size, room set and materials.

### Can you install a sauna anywhere in the regions?

Yes, we work across all of Azerbaijan, and we already have projects in Gabala, Gakh/Ilisu and Ismayilli.

### Can an outdoor sauna be used in a mountain winter?

Yes — winter is the favourite sauna season. Multilayer insulation, the membrane and a double-layer roof protect the sauna from cold and snow. In freezing weather, just make sure water lines and tubs don't freeze.

### Wood-burning or electric heater for a hotel?

For properties with high guest turnover, an electric heater with a separate control panel is more practical. If atmosphere is the priority and a chimney is possible, a wood-burning stove is a great choice too.
