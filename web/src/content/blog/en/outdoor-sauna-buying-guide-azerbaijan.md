Choosing an outdoor sauna in Azerbaijan comes down to five questions: how many people will use it at once, how much space you have, which shape you like, whether you want an electric or wood-burning stove, and what your budget is. This guide walks through each one so you end up with a sauna that suits your villa, holiday home or hotel garden for years to come.

## What an outdoor sauna is — and why it works so well here

An outdoor sauna is a standalone building in your garden rather than a room inside the house. The big advantage is that you get a full sauna without touching your home's walls, ventilation or floor plan. Step out of the heat straight into fresh air, a plunge tub or a pool, then head back in — that hot-cold rhythm is what most owners love.

### Barrel sauna

The classic. Its round cross-section lets hot air circulate evenly, so it heats up quickly. A great fit for families and small groups. See our [barrel sauna models](/en/barrel-sauna).

### Cube sauna

Clean lines and a contemporary look that pairs well with modern villas and minimalist landscaping. Same capacity and price range as the barrel.

### Semi-circular, elliptical and cabin saunas

For those who want more room inside. The cabin sauna's panoramic glass front brings the view into the sauna — ideal for plots overlooking mountains, forest or the Caspian.

### Cottage sauna and outdoor SPA complex

A house-shaped cottage sauna can combine several rooms: a sauna, steam room, salt room, Turkish hamam, broom (venik) room, massage room, hot or cold tub or pool, bathroom and a rest room. An outdoor SPA complex takes that further — a private spa at five-star hotel level, right in your own grounds. Popular with hotels, guesthouses and large family estates.

## Size and capacity

Count your household and your guests. As a rule of thumb:

- **2–4 people** — couples and small families; a compact barrel or cube is enough.
- **4–6 people** — a typical family with regular visitors; a longer barrel, cube or elliptical model.
- **6–8 people** — larger families and friends' weekends; a cabin or semi-circular sauna.
- **8+ people** — hotels, resorts and big villas; a cottage sauna or SPA complex.

Bigger is not automatically better. An oversized sauna takes longer to heat and uses more energy, so size for how you will really use it.

## Materials: what goes outside and inside

The exterior of an outdoor sauna faces rain, sun, frost and big temperature swings. We clad ours in water-resistant, thermo-treated **larch** (known locally as listvenica, red pine or "ship timber") — a wood with a long track record in demanding outdoor use.

Inside, the default is **linden (lipa)**: resin-free, smooth and light, and comfortable to touch even when hot. Cedar, abachi, African abachi and teak are available on request. We cover the pros and cons of each in a separate guide to sauna woods.

## Wall build-up: insulation and membrane

The quality of a sauna lives in the layers you never see. A properly built outdoor sauna wall is multilayered:

1. Exterior larch cladding.
2. Mineral stone wool insulation to keep heat in.
3. A special membrane that stops moisture and steam reaching the timber frame.
4. Interior lining in linden or the wood of your choice.

Barrel saunas add adjustable metal bands. Wood naturally shrinks a little as the weather changes; tightening the bands pulls the body snug again.

## Roofing: a double shield

The roof has to cope with downpours and snow. We lay **tegola** (bitumen shingles) over a heavy-duty **roofing felt** layer, creating a double shield against water. Ondulin or profiled metal sheet are available on other models, depending on the look and budget you prefer.

## Foundation and site preparation

Even a well-built sauna will struggle on a poorly prepared site. Plan for:

- **A level, solid base** — a concrete slab, paving, screw piles or a compacted gravel bed, depending on your soil.
- **Drainage** — water must not pool under or around the sauna; a slight fall or a drain helps.
- **Power** — an electric heater needs its own cable and breaker, planned with a qualified electrician.
- **Water and drainage lines** — if you want a shower, plunge tub or pool, plan the pipework early.
- **Access** — a route vehicles can reach makes bringing in materials and equipment far easier.

## Stove: electric or wood-burning?

| Factor | Electric heater | Wood-burning stove |
|---|---|---|
| Convenience | Push-button start, thermostat holds temperature | You load wood and tend the fire |
| Feel | Even, quiet heat | Crackling fire, traditional atmosphere |
| Requirements | Adequate electrical supply | Chimney and fire-safety measures |
| Best for | Frequent, shorter sessions | Country and mountain homes, purists |

## Planning your budget

The final figure depends on the model and size, the interior wood, the stove, any extra rooms (salt room, hamam, rest room), decor and lighting, and the site work. The prices in the table are approximate ranges; your exact quote is calculated for your project.

Tip: budget for more than the sauna itself — the base, the power line and accessories such as a bucket, ladle, thermometer and lighting all add up.

## Quick checklist before you buy

- How many people will use it at the same time?
- Have you measured the spot, including space to walk around it?
- Electric or wood-burning heat?
- Do you want a shower, plunge tub or rest area?
- Where will people cool down between rounds?

## FAQ

### Can I use an outdoor sauna in winter?

Yes. Multilayer insulation and a vapour membrane mean the sauna heats quickly and holds its warmth in cold weather. Many owners find winter is when an outdoor sauna is at its best.

### Do I need a foundation?

You need a level, solid base where water doesn't collect. A concrete slab, paving or screw piles all work; the right choice is best made after looking at your site.

### Barrel or cube sauna — which is better?

Both seat 2–6 people and sit in the same price range. The barrel offers the classic look and very even heat circulation; the cube offers modern lines and a slightly more practical interior layout.

### Which heater should I choose?

There is no single answer: electric is about convenience, wood is about atmosphere. What matters most is matching the heater's output to the sauna's volume.
