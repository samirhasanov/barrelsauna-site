The best wood for a sauna stays comfortable to touch when hot, releases no resin, copes with moisture and looks good for years. For walls and ceilings, **linden** is the go-to choice; for benches, **abachi** is hard to beat; **cedar** is chosen for its aroma; and **thermo linden** gives a darker, more moisture-resistant finish. On the outside of an outdoor sauna, water-resistant **larch** does the heavy lifting. Here's how they compare.

## Why the choice of wood matters

A sauna is not an ordinary room. Temperatures are high, humidity rises and falls, and your skin is in direct contact with the timber. The wrong wood can:

- feel uncomfortably hot against the skin;
- bleed resin onto the surface;
- warp, darken or rot from moisture;
- need frequent repairs or replacement.

So sauna timber is judged on four things: **heat conductivity** (the lower, the more comfortable to sit on), **resin content**, **resistance to moisture and fungus**, and **appearance and scent**.

## Interior sauna woods

### Linden (lipa)

The classic, all-round choice.

- Resin-free — nothing seeps out at high temperatures.
- Smooth, light and pale in colour.
- Doesn't burn the skin.
- Works for walls, ceiling and benches alike.

Linden is the standard interior wood in Barrel Sauna builds.

### Thermo linden

Thermo linden is linden that has been through a special high-temperature treatment. The result:

- better resistance to moisture, rot and warping;
- a rich, dark caramel colour that looks warm and premium;
- a light, pleasant scent.

A strong pick for contemporary designs and darker interiors.

### Abachi (ayous)

An African hardwood widely regarded as the world's number-one material for sauna benches, because:

- it has very low heat conductivity, so benches stay comfortable even in strong heat;
- it is knot-free with an even grain;
- it doesn't splinter.

Abachi is often used for benches, backrests and headrests, with another wood on the walls.

### Cedar

Cedar is all about aroma. Its natural essential oils give the sauna a gentle aromatherapy effect and are known for antibacterial properties. Cedar also:

- resists moisture, mould and fungus;
- has a rich, warm tone.

Ideal if you want a fragrant, close-to-nature atmosphere.

### The "wild sauna"

A "wild sauna" is built from whole linden logs and stumps. Instead of smooth panelling you get raw, natural texture — the room feels like a cabin in the woods. It's a striking option for homeowners who want something different, and for hotels and spas.

## Heat conductivity: why wood feels fine and metal burns

On a sauna bench you can sit comfortably on wood, yet touching a metal nail or handle at the same temperature can hurt. The reason is heat conductivity: metal transfers heat to your skin very quickly, wood does so slowly. The lighter and more porous the timber, the less heat it passes on — which is exactly why light, porous abachi is so prized for benches.

Two practical rules follow:

- bench fixings should be hidden, with no exposed metal on the surface;
- handles, backrests and headrests should be made from low-conductivity woods.

## Wood for outdoor saunas: outside vs inside

An outdoor sauna has two separate jobs. Inside, the criteria above apply. Outside, the timber has to withstand rain, sun and frost.

**Exterior — larch.** We clad our outdoor saunas in water-resistant, thermo-treated larch (listvenica), a red pine often called "ship timber" for how well it handles moisture.

**Interior** — linden by default, with cedar, abachi, African abachi or teak available. See all models on our [outdoor saunas](/en/outdoor-saunas) page.

## A wall build-up that protects the wood

Even the best timber won't last without the right wall behind it. Behind the panelling in our saunas you'll find:

- **mineral stone wool** to hold the heat;
- **a moisture-blocking membrane** that keeps steam away from the frame.

In saunas built inside a house, these layers also protect the home's own walls from steam and humidity. More on our [indoor saunas](/en/indoor-saunas) page.

## Quick recommendations

- **Classic, comfortable sauna** — linden.
- **Dark premium look plus extra moisture resistance** — thermo linden.
- **Most comfortable benches** — abachi, combined with another wood.
- **Aroma** — cedar.
- **Something truly different** — a wild sauna.
- **Outdoor sauna exterior** — larch.

Mixing woods works well: thermo linden walls with abachi benches, for example, look great and feel great.

## Looking after sauna wood

- Dry the room with the door open after every session.
- Sit on towels.
- Don't varnish or paint interior wood — it needs to breathe.
- Wipe benches with a clean damp cloth from time to time.

## FAQ

### What is the best wood for a sauna?

There's no single winner. Linden or thermo linden for walls and ceiling, abachi for benches and cedar for aroma are the most popular combinations.

### Why avoid resinous wood inside a sauna?

At high temperatures resin can bleed out and become sticky and hot on the skin. That's why resin-free woods such as linden and abachi are preferred indoors.

### How is thermo linden different from regular linden?

It is heat-treated, which makes it more resistant to moisture, rot and warping, and gives it a dark caramel colour and a light scent.

### Which wood is used on the outside of an outdoor sauna?

A water-resistant species. We use thermo-treated larch.

### What is a "wild sauna"?

A sauna built from whole linden logs and stumps, with a raw natural texture that feels like a forest cabin.
