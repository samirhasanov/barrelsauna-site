Hot tubs and jacuzzis both offer hot water for relaxation, but they do different jobs. The short answer: a **hot tub** is usually wooden, heated by wood or electricity, and gives a natural, rustic experience; a **jacuzzi** is an acrylic, electric system with hydromassage jets built for everyday comfort. There's also a third option — a **cold plunge** — for contrast after the sauna. Below we compare all three and explain how to pair them with a sauna.

## The three options in brief

### Wood-fired wooden hot tub

A large, round timber tub. The water is heated by a wood-burning stove or by electricity. Its big strength is atmosphere: sitting in hot water outdoors, with a hint of woodsmoke, snow or stars overhead. It matches an outdoor sauna visually and can run on the same energy source as the sauna heater.

### Electric acrylic jacuzzi

An acrylic tub powered by electricity. Water jets provide hydromassage, and temperature and lighting are adjustable. Press a button and the system does the rest. Ideal for daily relaxation and for anyone who wants the massage effect. It works outdoors as well as with indoor saunas and private spa zones.

### Cold plunge

A small tub filled with cold water. It's there to cool you down quickly after the sauna and deliver the contrast effect. Next to a sauna, it completes the "heat — cold — rest" cycle.

## Which to choose: real-life scenarios

### "I want to unwind in nature at my mountain house"

Go for a wooden hot tub. In our Gabala project, a wooden hot tub sits beside a barrel sauna in a snowy yard — a classic winter combination.

### "I want to relax every evening after work"

Choose a jacuzzi. It runs on electricity, there's no firewood to prepare, and hydromassage eases muscle tension. A convenient pick for a city villa or an Absheron dacha.

### "I want a proper cool-down after the sauna"

A cold plunge. Especially in summer, it works far better than a shower. If space allows, you can have both a cold plunge and a hot tub.

### "My yard is small and I want it all in one place"

A [container sauna + jacuzzi](/en/container-sauna-jacuzzi) puts the sauna, a jacuzzi or hot tub, a cold plunge and a lounge in a single module. It can be combined with a barrel, cube or cabin sauna.

## How to pair a sauna with a tub

A tub is the natural continuation of a sauna session. A typical cycle:

1. **Sauna** — 8–15 minutes.
2. **Cool down** — cold plunge, cool shower or fresh air.
3. **Rest** — a few minutes sitting down, with some water.
4. **Hot tub or jacuzzi** — relax your muscles at the end of the session or between rounds.

## Placement and installation

- **Distance.** Put the tub a step or two from the sauna door.
- **Foundation.** A filled tub is heavy, so it needs a solid, level base.
- **Water and drainage.** Plan the water supply and drain point in advance.
- **Power.** A suitably rated line for a jacuzzi or electric hot tub.
- **Wood stove.** For a wood-fired tub, consider smoke and chimney direction.
- **View and privacy.** You'll spend a lot of time in the tub — face it toward the best view.

## A quick word on safety

Avoid staying in hot water for too long, and don't combine it with alcohol. If you have a heart or blood-pressure condition, are pregnant, or have other health concerns, talk to your doctor before using hot or cold tubs. Children should only use a tub under adult supervision.
