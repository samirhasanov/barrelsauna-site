Hammam vs sauna comes down to one question: what kind of heat do you enjoy? A **Turkish hammam** is 40–50 °C with very high humidity, marble and warm stone. A **Finnish sauna** is 70–100 °C with dry heat in a timber room. A **steam room** is close to a hammam but simpler — a tiled room with a steam generator. Often the best answer is to combine them in one place, such as a cottage sauna or an outdoor spa complex.

## Three formats, three experiences

### Turkish hammam

A hammam is built around gentle, moist heat. The temperature is usually 40–50 °C with humidity close to 100%. At the centre is a heated marble platform, the **göbek taşı** ("navel stone"), where you lie to warm up and receive a foam massage and a kese scrub. Along the walls are **kurnas**, marble basins where hot and cold water are mixed and poured over the body. A hammam is about long, unhurried relaxation.

### Finnish sauna

A Finnish sauna is dry, intense heat: 70–100 °C with low humidity, usually 10–20%, rising briefly when water is thrown on the stones. The room is entirely wood — linden, abachi or cedar — with an electric or wood burning stove. Sessions are short (8–15 minutes) with cool-down breaks between them.

### Steam room

A steam room is essentially a simplified hammam: 40–50 °C and around 100% humidity. Steam comes from a separate **steam generator**, the walls are finished in tile, mosaic or stone, and benches are often heated. It can be built without the navel stone and basins, so it takes up less space.

| Parameter | Turkish hammam | Finnish sauna | Steam room |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature | 40–50 °C | 70–100 °C | 40–50 °C |
| Humidity | ~100% | 10–20% (rises with löyly) | ~100% |
| Heat source | Heated platform, walls and floor, plus steam | Electric or wood stove with stones | Steam generator |
| Interior finish | Marble, stone, ceramics | Wood (linden, abachi, cedar) | Tile, mosaic, stone |
| Session | Long and calm (30+ minutes) | Short rounds + cool-down | Medium |
| Feel | Soft, moist, relaxing | Strong, dry, invigorating | Soft, moist |

## What matters when building a hammam

Hammams and steam rooms are technically more demanding than saunas because they run at near-100% humidity all the time.

- **Waterproofing and vapour barrier.** Walls, floor and ceiling must stop water and steam completely, or moisture gets into the structure.
- **Ceiling shape.** A dome or sloped ceiling stops condensation dripping on bathers — water runs down the walls instead.
- **Heating.** The platform, benches and floor are warmed by a hydronic or electric system, while a generator supplies steam.
- **Drainage.** The floor slopes towards a drain.
- **Ventilation.** The room has to dry out quickly after use.
- **Wet-rated lighting and electrics.** Equipment designed for wet rooms only.

A sauna's main challenge is different: thermal insulation. Barrel Sauna's wall system — mineral stone wool plus a special moisture-proof membrane — keeps steam out of the timber frame and helps the sauna heat up faster.

## Which one is right for you?

- **You love strong heat and want to feel energised** — Finnish sauna.
- **Gentle warmth, long relaxation, massage** — Turkish hammam.
- **Limited space, simpler build, moist steam** — steam room.
- **Different tastes in the family** — a combined solution.

If you have a heart condition, blood pressure issues, are pregnant or have other health concerns, talk to your doctor before using any heat therapy.

## Combining a sauna and a hammam

The most popular approach isn't to build a hammam on its own, but to make it part of one relaxation space.

### Cottage sauna

A [cottage sauna](/en/cottage-sauna) is a house-shaped outdoor building that can combine a sauna room, steam room, salt room, Turkish hammam, a venik (whisking) room, massage room, hot/cold tub or pool, bathroom/WC and a lounge. It holds 4–20 people, with an approximate price of 10,000–50,000 AZN depending on the rooms and size. You can see our İsmayıllı cottage and other work on the [projects](/en/projects) page.

### Outdoor spa complex

An [outdoor spa complex](/en/outdoor-spa-complex) is the idea of a "5-star hotel level private spa in your yard". The same set of rooms — sauna, hammam, steam room, pool or hot tub and a lounge — comes together in a single project. It holds 4–15 people, with an approximate price of 15,000–45,000 AZN. ## FAQ

### What's the main difference between a hammam and a sauna?

Temperature and humidity. A hammam runs at 40–50 °C with very high humidity; a sauna at 70–100 °C with dry heat. Materials differ too: a hammam uses marble and stone, a sauna uses wood.

### Can a steam room replace a hammam?

They feel similar — both give soft, moist heat. But the classic hammam ritual needs a heated navel stone and basins, which steam rooms usually don't have.

### Can a sauna and a hammam share one building?

Yes. That's exactly what cottage saunas and outdoor spa complexes are for: sauna, hammam, steam room and lounge in one project.

### What materials are used to build a hammam?

Marble, natural stone, ceramics and mosaic, over waterproofing and a vapour barrier. Wood is rarely used inside a hammam because of the constant humidity.

### How much does a cottage sauna cost?

Approximately 10,000–50,000 AZN. The price depends on the number of rooms, size, materials and equipment.
