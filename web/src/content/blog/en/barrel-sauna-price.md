## What drives the price

### 1. Length and capacity

A barrel's diameter stays roughly the same; what changes capacity is mainly the **length of the body**. A longer barrel needs more timber, more insulation, more steel bands and a more powerful stove. That's why capacity is the single biggest price factor.

A practical tip: size the sauna for how many people usually bathe together, not for the biggest party you might host once a year. Extra volume costs money and takes longer to heat.

### 2. Interior wood

The exterior is the same on every barrel — water-resistant, thermally treated larch ("listvenica", often called ship timber). The difference is the **interior lining**:

- **Linden (lipa)** — the default: resin-free, smooth, light-coloured, never burns your skin.
- **Cedar** — natural essential oils, a lovely scent and antibacterial properties.
- **Abachi and African abachi** — very low heat conductivity, the go-to bench material.
- **Teak** — a premium look and excellent durability.

Premium wood raises the price, but you don't have to upgrade everything. A smart compromise is linden walls with abachi benches — the benches are what your body actually touches.

### 3. Stove type

A barrel sauna can run on an **electric** or a **wood-burning** stove. Electric is the most convenient for frequent use; wood gives a classic bathhouse atmosphere and doesn't need a heavy-duty power line. Barrel Sauna supplies and installs Harvia, SAWO, Teplodar, TMF and Fanlan stoves, with output matched to the sauna's volume. Browse them on the [sauna heaters](/en/sauna-heaters) page.

### 4. Glass

A classic barrel has a small window in the door or back wall. A **panoramic glass back wall** or a fully glazed front changes the whole experience: you sit on the bench and look out at your garden, the mountains or the forest. It costs more, but on a site with a view it's one of the upgrades that pays off most.

### 5. Front terrace

On some barrels, part of the body is set aside as a small covered terrace in front of the door — a place to cool down between rounds and leave towels and clothes. A terrace lengthens the body, so it affects the price.

### 6. Extras

- LED lighting
- Decor: decorative stones, Himalayan salt panels
- Accessories: bucket, ladle, thermometer, sauna hats

## What's worth paying for

**Always worth it:**

- **A correctly sized stove.** Too weak won't reach sauna temperature; too strong is money wasted.
- **Good bench wood.** It's where comfort is felt most.
- **Glass, if you have a view.** Mountains or a garden behind glass transform every session.

**Can be added later:**

- Decor and most accessories.
- Extra lighting scenes.

**Where not to cut corners:** the body construction. Every Barrel Sauna barrel has a multilayer wall (mineral stone wool + a moisture-proof membrane), a double-shield roof (tegola shingles over roofing felt) and adjustable steel bands that let you re-tighten the body as the wood naturally shrinks with the seasons.

## Costs to plan for beyond the sauna

- **Base.** The sauna sits on a level, solid, well-drained surface: a concrete slab, paving stones or screw piles. If you already have a suitable pad, this may cost nothing.
- **Power.** An electric stove needs a suitable supply line.
- **Cool-down zone.** A shower, a cold plunge or a hot tub — many owners add one next to the barrel.

## What to check if you find a cheaper offer

You'll come across lower prices. When comparing quotes, look beyond the headline figure at what's actually included:

- **What is the body made of?** Outdoors you need water-resistant, thermally treated timber.
- **Is there insulation and a membrane in the wall?** A single layer of boards won't hold heat in winter.
- **How is the roof covered?** A double shield against rain and snow means a longer life.
- **Are the bands adjustable?** To compensate for natural wood shrinkage.
- **Which stove brand, and is its output right for the volume?**

Two barrels at the same price can answer these questions very differently — and a few years on, that's where the difference shows.
