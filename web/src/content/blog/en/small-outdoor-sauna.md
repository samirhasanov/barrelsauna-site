A small outdoor sauna is a compact two-person sauna that fits a small yard, a terrace or a corner of a country plot. The most popular formats are the barrel and the cube: both can be built for 2–6 people, and their smallest versions are made exactly for couples and small families. Expect roughly 6,000–12,000 AZN. Below we look at which shape suits you, how much space you need and why a compact sauna works so well.

## Who a small sauna is for

- **Owners of small yards** — when there's no room for a cottage sauna or spa complex.
- **Couples and small families** — if mostly one or two people use the sauna.
- **Homes with a terrace** — for a sauna right next to the house.
- **Daily sauna users** — a simple sauna that heats quickly after work.
- **Country and mountain house owners** — a compact standalone building for weekends.

## Compact formats: which one?

### Barrel sauna

The barrel is the classic small sauna. Its round body has no dead corners, so the volume to heat is small and it warms up fast. Adjustable metal bands let you re-tighten the body when the timber naturally shrinks with changes in the weather. More on our [barrel sauna](/en/barrel-sauna) page.

### Cube sauna

A square-edged shape uses the interior more efficiently: benches sit neatly along flat walls and every corner is usable. A cube also fits more easily at the edge of a terrace or along a fence. The cube sauna we built in the mountains of Gakh (Ilisu) is a great example. More on our [cube sauna](/en/cube-sauna) page.

### Portable sauna

If you'd like to move your sauna — say, between a country house and a city yard — look at the [portable sauna](/en/portable-sauna). It's a flexible option that doesn't need a permanent foundation.

### Cabin sauna

The cabin, with its panoramic glass front, looks stunning but is designed for 4–8 people. If your brief is "small, but with guests now and then", it's the next step up.

## How much space do you need?

When planning a spot for a small sauna, look beyond the sauna's own footprint. Allow for:

1. **Door swing** — the door should open fully, with room to stand comfortably at the entrance.
2. **Heater and chimney clearances** — with a wood-fired heater, keep a safe distance from the chimney to the house, trees and neighbouring structures.
3. **Service access** — even a narrow path around it, so you can inspect the body and roof.
4. **A place to cool down** — a small bench or an outdoor shower.
5. **The base** — level, solid and free-draining: a concrete slab, paving or screw piles.

### A sauna on a terrace

You can put a sauna on a timber deck, but first check that the deck can take the load: the sauna, heater stones and people inside add up to a serious weight. If a wood-fired sauna sits close to the house, fire-safety clearances matter even more.

## Why go small

- **Heats up fast.** A smaller volume reaches temperature sooner.
- **Uses less energy.** A low-output heater is enough for a small room.
- **Takes little space.** Ideal for small yards and terraces.
- **Made for daily use.** Easy preparation means you'll actually use it often.
- **Friendlier budget.** A lower starting price than larger models.

## Choosing a heater for a small sauna

A small room doesn't need a powerful heater — in fact, an oversized heater makes the heat harsh. Small saunas usually do well with a low-output electric heater (roughly 3–4.5 kW); the exact rating is calculated from the room volume, and the circuit should be confirmed by a qualified electrician. Compact wood-fired heaters are available too. Barrel Sauna sells and installs Harvia, SAWO and Saunabee heaters — see the options on our [sauna heaters](/en/sauna-heaters) page.

## Tips for everyday use

- **Air it after every session.** Moisture builds up quickly in a small room — leave the door open for a while so the timber dries.
- **Sit on a towel.** In a compact sauna you're in closer contact with the wood, and towels keep the benches clean.
- **Keep rounds short.** A small sauna heats fast and the heat can feel more intense — listen to your body.
- **Check the bands and roof.** When the seasons change, look over the barrel's bands and the roof covering.
- **Light the path.** A short, lit path from the house makes evening and winter sessions much easier.

If you have health concerns — especially heart conditions — or are pregnant, talk to your doctor before using a sauna.

## FAQ

### How much does a 2-person outdoor sauna cost?

Barrel and cube saunas cost roughly 6,000–12,000 AZN. The final price depends on size, interior timber, heater and extras.

### Can I put a small sauna on a terrace?

Yes, once the deck's load capacity has been checked. With a wood-fired heater you also need a safe distance to the house.

### Barrel or cube — which is better for a small yard?

The barrel heats faster and has a distinctive look; the cube uses interior space more efficiently and lines up neatly with straight boundaries. It comes down to your yard's shape and your taste.

### Can a small sauna be used in winter?

Yes. The walls are insulated and have a membrane, so even a compact sauna heats quickly and holds warmth well in winter.

### Does a small sauna need a foundation?

It needs a level, solid base — a concrete slab, paving or screw piles. The right option is chosen after seeing the site.
